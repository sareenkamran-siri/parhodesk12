How weighted grades actually work

ParhoDesk guide · Updated October 1, 2026

Why a simple average can give the wrong answer

Imagine two assessments: a quiz worth 20% of a course and an exam worth 80%. You score 80 on the quiz and 90 on the exam. Averaging the scores equally produces 85, but it treats the small quiz as if it mattered as much as the exam. The course average is 88 because the larger assessment has more influence. Weighting describes that influence; it is not a bonus added to your mark.

Calculate the contribution first

Multiply each percentage score by its weight expressed as a decimal. The quiz contributes 80 × 0.20 = 16 percentage points. The exam contributes 90 × 0.80 = 72 percentage points. Add the contributions: 16 + 72 = 88. Alternatively, multiply scores by whole-number weights and divide their sum by 100. Keep the same convention throughout the calculation.

When only some results are available

Suppose you have a quiz worth 20% with a score of 80 and coursework worth 30% with a score of 70. Their contributions are 16 and 21 points. You have earned 37 course points from assessments covering 50% of the course. Your average across completed work is 37 ÷ 0.50 = 74%. That is different from saying your final course grade is 74: half the course has not yet been assessed.

Missing is different from zero

Leave a future assessment out when finding the average of completed work. Enter zero only if the assessment has actually earned zero and your course rules count it that way. Some schools drop a lowest quiz, replace an exam mark, or use category averages. In those cases, calculate the category score according to the syllabus before entering its course weight.

Use the number to plan

A strong average in a small category cannot always offset a weak heavily weighted exam. List the remaining assessments, their weights, and realistic targets. Use the final exam calculator if only one assessment remains. If the target requires more than 100%, change the target or check whether a formal reassessment option exists; do not assume extra credit.

A quick checking routine

Ensure weights total 100% for a complete course. Convert raw marks to percentages first: 18 out of 25 is 72%, not 18%. Round at the end unless your institution tells you otherwise. Keep the syllabus beside your calculations and compare against official results. This tool estimates percentages; only your institution can award a final grade.
