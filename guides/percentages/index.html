Percentages, percentage points, and changing bases

ParhoDesk guide · Updated October 1, 2026

A percentage needs a base

A percentage means a number of parts out of 100. Twenty percent of 150 is 0.20 × 150 = 30. The base is 150. Whenever a percentage is quoted, ask what quantity it refers to. The same 20% can mean a very different amount when the underlying total changes.

Change uses the original value

If a score rises from 40 to 50, the increase is 10. Divide that increase by the original 40: 10 ÷ 40 × 100 = 25%. If it falls from 50 to 40, the decrease is 10 ÷ 50 × 100 = 20%. Equal numerical differences need not produce equal percentage changes because the base is different.

Percentage points are different

A pass rate moving from 60% to 75% rises by 15 percentage points. Its relative percentage increase is 15 ÷ 60 × 100 = 25%. Both statements can be correct, but they describe different quantities. In a report, name the measure explicitly so readers do not confuse a subtraction of rates with a relative increase.

Successive changes compound

A 20% reduction followed by a 20% increase does not return you to the start. Begin at 100: the reduction leaves 80, then the increase adds 16, giving 96. For successive changes, multiply by each factor in order. Here those factors are 0.8 and 1.2. Do not simply add the percentages.

Watch for zero and negative bases

Percentage change from zero is undefined because the calculation divides by zero. A negative original value can yield a mathematically valid but confusing sign in everyday descriptions. Explain the absolute change instead, or use a subject-specific convention and name it. ParhoDesk’s change tool requires a positive original value to avoid implying an unambiguous interpretation.

Check with an estimate

Before trusting a decimal result, estimate the scale. Ten percent of 240 is 24, so 25% should be 60. A result of 6 or 600 suggests a misplaced decimal. Keep precision during intermediate steps and round the final result to match the context. Use the percentage calculator to check your arithmetic, then state the base in your answer.
